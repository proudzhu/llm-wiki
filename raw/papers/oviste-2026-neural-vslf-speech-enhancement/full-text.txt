Abstract—Leveraging the predictive capabilities of neural networks to
estimate intermediate parameters to derive a linear filter has proven to be
an effective and robust approach to multi-channel speech enhancement.
Here, we adopt the parameterized Variable Span Linear Filter (VSLF)
framework, which provides a generalization of classical beamformer
designs like the Multi-Channel Wiener Filter or Minimum Variance
Distortionless Response. We propose an architecture that uses a deep
neural network (DNN) to predict three intermediate quantities – both
the clean-speech and overall-noise spatial covariance matrices, and an
optimal speech-distortion tradeoff parameter – to compute the complex
VSLF weights. Experiments show that our method improves speech intelligibility and quality over end-to-end baselines, while enabling explicit
control of the tradeoff between speech distortion and noise reduction.
Index Terms—multi-channel speech enhancement, spatial covariance
matrix, optimal linear filter, supervised learning.

I. I NTRODUCTION
Multi-channel speech enhancement is an essential task for a
wide variety of downstream applications, including distant speech
recognition, hearing aids, teleconferences and own-voice enhancement. The task consists in extracting a clean speech signal from a
noisy environment contaminated with background noise, interfering
speakers and reverberation in the room. Typically, a user could be
located in a call center or an open office, in an online meeting, have
their voice captured by true-wireless earbuds, and wish to transmit
only their speech, free of the surrounding noise.
Existing methods of multi-channel speech enhancement can generally be grouped into three categories [1], [2]: i) linear filtering
methods driven by probabilistic models, such as the Multi-Channel
Wiener Filter (MWF) [3], the Minimum Variance Distortionless
Response (MVDR) [4], [5] or the Generalized Eigenvalue (GEV)
beamformer [6], with optional post-filtering [7]; ii) end-to-end datadriven approaches [8]–[11]; and iii) hybrid methods [12]–[14], in
which a generally smaller neural network is leveraged to guide
a multi-channel filter by estimating the second order statistics of
speech and noise. Recently, hybrid methods have gained interest
thanks to their explainability [14], [15], their ability to allow control
over the intermediate parameters [16], and their robustness due to
incorporating physical assumptions that regularize the components
estimated by the DNN [1].
A central objective in speech enhancement is managing the tradeoff
between noise reduction and speech distortion. While this tradeoff can
be explicitly formulated and controlled in traditional linear filtering,
for instance with the speech-distortion weighted MWF [17]–[19], it
is implicitly learned and generally uncontrollable in end-to-end datadriven approaches, resulting in undesirable, hard-to-quantify artifacts.
Consequently, recent hybrid methods aim to mitigate these artifacts
by using a DNN to guide a linear filter, thereby re-introducing explicit
control. Such approaches include combining a DNN with a multiframe MWF [20], dynamically parameterizing the MWF to control
the tradeoff [16], refining the filter output with a second DNN [21],
or learning the multi-channel speech presence probability to guide
the filtering process [13].

Despite these advances, hybrid methods rarely employ filter formulations other than MWF or MVDR. We believe this limits the
potential of the approach, and argue that it can be broadened to
other linear filter designs, and that alternative analyses of the digital
signal processing (DSP)-related quantities estimated by the DNN can
be investigated. To this end, we adopt the Variable Span Linear
Filter (VSLF) framework developed in [22], [23]. This framework
derives optimal linear filters with a controllable tradeoff between
speech distortion and noise reduction. Notably, it also generalizes
the MWF, MVDR, and GEV beamformers, treating them as special
cases, providing a parameterized filter that adapts to the objective
of the system and the characteristics of the input signal. Because
computing this filter requires spatial covariance matrices (SCMs) and
an optimal speech-distortion tradeoff parameter whose true values are
generally unknown, we employ a DNN to estimate these quantities
through supervised training with clean-noisy speech signal pairs.
The novelty of this work is twofold: 1) the integration, for the
first time, of the VSLF framework into a DNN-guided architecture,
dubbed Hybrid Variable Span Filter (HVSF); and 2) the study of
the parameters estimated by the trained HVSF, showing how it offers
interpretability of the rank of the clean-speech SCM and control over
the filter’s performance in noise attenuation and speech preservation.

background noise. The noisy mixture is recorded by M microphones,

E. Estimating the Speech-Distortion Tradeoff Parameter
The DNN output zµ is constrained via exponentiation to provide
a positive tradeoff parameter: µ̂ = 10zµ ∈ R>0 . This formulation
allows µ̂ to span a wide dynamic range, including very small values
effectively near zero. Indeed, experiments with oracle VSLFs revealed
that the generalized eigenvalues can exhibit a large dynamic range.
As their value is modulated by addition with µ̂ (see Eq. (12)) to
control the noise reduction, µ̂ must be similarly flexible.
IV. E XPERIMENTS
A. Dataset
We create synthetic pairs of multi-channel clean and noisy speech
signals, relying on the pyroomacoustics [27] library. We design
acoustic scenes as follows: a room of length 4–10 meters, of width
4–10 meters, of height 3–5 meters, of reverberation time 0–1 second,
is simulated. We place a target speech source randomly in the room,
at least 1 meter away from the walls, at height 1–2 meters, to emit
a signal taken from the CSTR VCTK corpus [28]. We also place an
interfering speech source randomly in the room, at least 1 meter away
from the walls, at height 1–2 meters, at least 1 meter away from the
target speech source, to emit a signal taken from the LibriSpeech
corpus [29]. We position an array of M = 4 microphones a
few centimeters above and behind the target speech source, and
rotate it at random. This simulates true-wireless earbuds applications,
where the target speaker is wearing the device. We generate multichannel mixed-speech signals using the image method [30] from
pyroomacoustics, with order and absorption coefficient defined
by the inverse Sabine equation. Additionally, we construct multichannel ambient noise signals by sampling random clips from singlechannel noise recordings of the DEMAND corpus [31], treating these
clips as M = 4 uncorrelated channels, thereby generating a diverse
set of multi-channel realizations. We force spatial coherence among
channels using the generator defined in [32], and add the ambient

V. R ESULTS AND D ISCUSSIONS
The first experiment is a proof of concept where we visualize
intermediate quantities of the system to get insight into how the
HVSF operates. Looking at Fig. 2.d., we see that the method tends
to assign a larger span dimension Q – corresponding to the number

and noise-reduction performance of the trained FT-JNF (HVSF) model.

of generalized eigenvalues above the fixed threshold τ = 1.0 – to
TF bins where speech is present. Additionally, looking at Fig. 2.e.,
we see that the method tends to assign a larger tradeoff parameter µ̂
– corresponding to more noise reduction at the cost of more speech
distortion – to TF bins where speech is absent.
We also report the performance obtained by the models trained
both individually and within the HVSF framework, averaged over
our test set, in Table I. Results show that the integration into the
HVSF framework improves performance across all metrics for the
two studied architectures, demonstrating the benefit of our hybrid
method for speech enhancement. We also measure the computational
cost of the framework, due to the addition of DSP blocks and the
modification of the original DNN architecture. Notably, for FT-JNF,
only the output size of one final linear layer needs to be increased,
keeping the added complexity to a minimum.
Our last experiment demonstrates that the proposed method allows
to manually control the tradeoff between speech distortion and noise
reduction. Here, various multipliers are applied to the DNN-estimated
parameter µ̂ before computing the filter weights. Results are displayed
in Fig. 3. It shows that as we scale the tradeoff parameter up, the
method’s noise-reduction performance increases, at the cost of more
distorted speech. Audio examples can be found on the webpage that
accompanies this paper5 .
VI. C ONCLUSION
We proposed HVSF, a multi-channel speech-enhancement method
that leverages a low-parameter DNN to guide a variable span linear
filter. The DNN learns to estimate three quantities, namely the cleanspeech SCM, the overall-noise SCM, and a speech-distortion tradeoff
parameter, to determine the filter weights. Our method proved to be
model-agnostic, systematically improving the performance of models
from the literature compared to their end-to-end original counterpart,
and demonstrated its potential in interpretability and control over the
intermediate quantities estimated by the DNN.

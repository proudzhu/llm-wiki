ABSTRACT
Although deep learning (DL) based end-to-end models have shown
outstanding performance in multi-channel speech extraction, their
practical applications on edge devices are restricted due to their high
computational complexity. In this paper, we propose a hybrid system
that can more effectively integrate the generalized sidelobe canceller
(GSC) and a lightweight post-filtering model under the assistance of
spatial speaker activity information provided by a directional voice
activity detection (DV AD) module. In addition to guiding the update
of the adaptive blocking matrix (ABM) and the adaptive interference
canceller (AIC) used in GSC to alleviate the distortion of the de-
sired speech, DV AD is also utilized as an auxiliary input to the post-
filtering model to enhance its capability of interference suppression.
The experimental results demonstrate that, with much lower com-
putational costs, our method can achieve comparable performance
with a current state-of-the-art end-to-end model on simulated data
and generalize even better on real-world data.
Index Terms— Multi-channel, target speaker extraction, gener-
alized sidelobe canceller, directional voice activity detection
1. INTRODUCTION
The target speaker’s speech is often corrupted by competing speakers
and background noise in practical applications, resulting in a decline
in communication quality and speech recognition accuracy. Target
speaker extraction (TSE) aims at extracting desired speech in such
an environment, which is achieved by utilizing auxiliary cues related
to the desired speaker. For example, speaker embeddings used in
personalized speech enhancement [1, 2, 3] are an effective feature to
discriminate speakers. However, to produce the embedding vector,
an enrollment utterance from the target speaker is required, which
might be inaccessible in many cases. When the location of the target
speaker is known, as in many video conferencing systems, multiple
microphones’ spatial information can be exploited to help extract the
desired speech.
For an array with only a small number of microphones, the nor-
mally utilized linear beamforming [4] suffers from limited interfer-
ence suppression, and a well-designed post-filter (PF) is required.
The generalized sidelobe canceller (GSC) [5] is a well-known strat-
egy that can further suppress interference by the implementation
of an adaptive interference canceller (AIC). However, the desired
speech is often inevitably distorted due to the leakage of the desired
signal in the blocking matrix (BM) module [6]. In order to tackle
this problem, the robust GSC proposed in [7] controls the weights
update of the adaptive BM (ABM) and the AIC by a signal-to-noise
ratio (SNR) estimate. In [8] Hoshuyama et al. uses an ABM with
coefficient-constrained and an AIC with norm-constrained adaptive
filters. Moreover, in [9] and [10] the ABM is assisted with voice ac-
tivity detection (V AD) based on signal energy and a recurrent neural
network (RNN). However, these methods assume the existence of a
single speaker, and their performance will deteriorate in scenarios
with interfering speakers.
The data-driven deep neural networks (DNN) based approaches
have been proposed in recent years for multi-channel TSE. For ex-
ample, neural beamformers [11, 12, 13] preserve the structure of
conventional beamformers and estimate the spatial covariance matri-
ces (SCMs) of the target speech (or the noise) with neural networks.
In contrast, deep non-linear filters [14, 15, 16] jointly process tempo-
ral, spectral and spatial information, and directly estimate the com-
plex spectrogram of target speech from multi-channel mixture. Al-
though these methods make great progress in speech enhancement,
the high computational complexity makes them difficult for appli-
cation in real-time audio processing. Meanwhile, the robustness of
end-to-end approaches is not guaranteed in unseen environments.
In this paper, we propose a lightweight hybrid multi-channel
TSE system by integrating a robust GSC with a DNN-based PF.
A lightweight convolutional recurrent network (CRN) is employed
to estimate the directional V AD (DV AD) of each speaker. The
DV AD in the target speaker’s direction is used to assist ABM and
AIC in GSC to enhance the performance of GSC. Then the fixed
beamformer (FBF) output, the GSC output and the complete DV AD
are fed into the PF. Experimental results have shown that, with
much lower complexity our approach can achieve competitive per-
formance with the current state-of-the-art end-to-end method on
synthetic data, and even generalize better on real-world data.

2.4. DPCRN based post-filter
The dual-path CRN (DPCRN) [21] is a lightweight speech enhance-
ment network, ranking the second in the wide band scenario track
of the Interspeech 2021 Deep Noise Suppression (DNS) challenge
[22]. As depicted in Fig. 1, it consists of a convolutional en-
coder/decoder and a dual-path RNN (DPRNN) [23]. In the DPRNN
module, the intra-frame RNN block models the spectral patterns in
a single frame, and the inter-frame RNN block models the time de-
pendency. Although suppressing more interference, the AIC output

3. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
3.1. Datasets
We generate the simulated dataset using the source-image model
[25]. Referring to the data preparation procedure in [26], the room
size ranges uniformly from 3m×2.5m×2.2m to 9m×5m×3.5m and
the reverberation time RT60 ranges uniformly from 0.4s to 1.2s. We
use a uniform six-microphone circular array of radius 3.5 cm, which
is placed randomly in the room at the height of 1m and at least 1m
from the walls. As depicted in Fig. 2. (a), the horizontal plane is di-
vided into six zones uniformly, with each zone covering a 60◦-span
area. We choose the first microphone as the reference channel and
zone-1 as the target zone, which corresponds to an azimuthal range
from−30◦ to 30◦ relative to the first microphone. As depicted by
the blue dashed line, the target speaker is positioned between−20◦
and 20◦, to ensure the absence of interfering speakers within a spec-
ified range (10◦).
With a length of 12s and a sampling rate of 16 kHz, the multi-
channel mixture sample consists of reverberant speech from between
1 and 3 speakers and diffuse noise. The distance between the micro-
phone array and the speakers ranges from 0.5m to 5m, and the speak-
ers are at least 0.5m away from the walls. The heights of the speakers
are sampled from a normal distribution with mean 1.3m and stan-
dard deviation 0.08m. We use clean speech data from Librispeech
[27] and noise data from QUT-NOISE [28], Nonspeech [29] and the
noise subset in MUSAN [30]. The approach in [31] is employed to
generate diffuse noise. The SNR is sampled between 3 dB and 12
dB, and the signal-to-interference ratio (SIR) is sampled betwen -3
dB and 3 dB. Because the DV AD module and the PF are trained sep-
arately, two datasets are generated from the same source data. Each
dataset consists of 4000, 1000, 500 RIRs and 30000, 5000 and 1500
samples, respectively for training, validation and testing.
3.1.1. Dataset for directional VAD
In the samples for training the DV AD module, the azimuths of the
speakers are uniformly sampled and the speech duration ranges from
4s to 6s. The ground-truth V AD labels of multi-speakers are obtained
by applying the rV AD algorithm [32] to reverberant speech of each
speaker before mixing and then converted to DV AD ones. An exam-
ple of the DV AD label is shown in Fig. 2. (b).
3.1.2. Dataset for post-filtering
There exists a target speaker and between 0 and 2 competing speak-
ers in each sample for training the PF. The azimuth of the target
speaker is sampled from a normal distribution with mean 0◦ and
standard deviation 10◦, and limited to a range between −20◦ and
20◦. The competing speakers are restricted outside zone-1. Speech
duration of the target speaker is modified to be between 6s and 8s.
Moreover, we recorded half an hour of real-world noisy test data in
a small conference room using a microphone array identical to the
simulated setup. Yamaha HS5 monitoring loudspeakers were placed

3.2. Model setup
A square root Hanning window is applied to the multi-channel mix-
ture signal with a 32ms frame length and a 16ms frame shift. The
STFT results contain F =257 frequency-bins per frame. The FBF
employed in our method is a robust super-directive beamformer [34].
In the DV AD module, all CNN layers are configured with 16 chan-
nels and the kernel size is set as{(3,3),(3,1),(3,1)} in the frequency
and time axes. Each of the four GRUs in the RNN layer has 32 hid-
den units, while the output dimensions of the FC layers are 36 and 6.
The DV AD module has approximately 33K parameters in total and
a computational complexity of 31M MACs per second.
For the PF we adopt the same model configuration as that of
DPCRN-3 in [21], with the exception of removing the instance layer
normalization (iLN) module and modifying the hidden dimension
within the DPRNN modules from 128 to 134 due to the concatenated
DV AD tensor. Consequently, these modifications result in the PF
having about 0.84M parameters and a computation load of 1.79G
MACs per second. To ensure the causality of our method, causal
convolutions and uni-directional inter-frame RNNs are employed in
the modules.

4. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
We evaluate the DV AD module on the test set for DV AD and the
model achieves its best performance with precision of 94% and recall
of 95% when Pthreshold set to 0.5. This threshold is selected for the
subsequenet experiments and an example from the test set for post-
filtering is displayed in Fig. 2. (c). Four types of PFs have been
evaluated on the simulated test set, each with its respective input
features as follows:
• PF-mono: GSC output.
• PF-dual: FBF output and GSC output.
• PF-DV AD-1ch: FBF output, GSC output and DV AD in zone-1.
• PF-DV AD-6ch: FBF output, GSC output and DV AD in all zones.
4.1. Baseline and evaluation metrics
We compare our method with a recently proposed end-to-end model
FT-JNF [26], which is implemented with the open-source code1 and
the inter-frame bi-directional long-short-term memory (BLSTM)
layer is replaced by a uni-directional one to satisfy causality in our
experiments. Objective metrics are used, including scale-invariant
signal-to-distortion ratio (SI-SDR) [36], perceptual evaluation of
speech quality (PESQ) [37] and extended short-time objective in-
telligibility (ESTOI) [38]. DNN-based non-intrusive subjective
metrics DNSMOS P.835 [39] is also used to evaluate subject speech
performance both on the simulated and the real-world test set.
4.2. Results
We train all networks using the same training set. As is presented
in Table 1, the simple monaural PF represented by PF-mono, has
achieved a significant improvement of over 10 dB in SI-SDR. How-
ever, a substantial performance gap exists when compared to FT-
JNF. The introduction of FBF output and DV AD information pro-
gressively enhances the PF’s performance. With the inclusion of
FBF output, speech distortion caused by the GSC can be effectively
compensated for, resulting in evident improvements across all objec-
tive metrics. PF-dual outperforms FT-JNF in terms of PESQ and ES-
TOI scores. With the assistance of DV AD, even better performance
can be achieved by PF-DV AD-1ch and PF-DV AD-6ch. Compared to

5. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we propose a hybrid multi-channel target speaker ex-
traction system, which combines the robust GSC with a dedicated
neural post-filter. We design a DV AD module to fully exploit the spa-
tial information of multi-speakers. The DV AD results are not only
utilized to enhance the GSC output, but also incorporated as auxil-
iary information to assist interference suppression in the post-filter.
Experimental results demonstrate that our proposed method achieves
comparable performance to a state-of-the-art end-to-end method on
simulated data, and outperforms it on real-world data.

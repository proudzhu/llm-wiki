Faculty of Engineering Science, Kansai University, 3-3-35 Yamate-cho, Suita-shi, 564-8680, Osaka, Japan

Abstract
This paper proposes a hybrid active noise control system (ANC) for remote microphone-based virtual sensing with two compensation filters. The proposed system can accurately estimate the desired signal at the desired location by creating compensation filters for multiple noise sources. Two compensation filters are created for each primary path from the noise source to the error microphone and the desired point in feed-forward and feedback ANC. An additional adaptive filter decomposes the mixed signal captured by the error microphone into individual source components. Each component is then processed using its corresponding compensation filter in both feedforward and feedback control, allowing the system to reconstruct the desired signal with high accuracy. Simulation results using measured impulse responses demonstrate that the proposed system improves noise reduction by approximately 20 dB compared to the conventional hybrid ANC system.
Keywords: Active noise control, Hybrid ANC system, Virtual sensing, Remote microphone-based virtual sensing

1. Intorduction
In recent years, noise emissions from factories and industrial facilities have become a serious social concern in urban and densely populated industrial areas. Such noise not only degrades the working environment but also adversely affects the health and quality of life of nearby residents. As a result, there is an increasing demand for effective noise control strategies.

Conventional noise mitigation approaches have largely relied on passive noise control techniques such as sound-insulation barriers and absorptive materials. However, these methods are often ineffective for low-frequency noise and in large, reverberant spaces with spatially distributed noise sources. To overcome these limitations, active noise control (ANC) [? ? ? ? ? ] has gained considerable attention, especially in industrial settings characterized by the simultaneous operation of multiple machinery-based noise sources.
ANC systems attenuate unwanted noise by generating anti-noise signals that are equal in amplitude and opposite in phase, effectively canceling the noise through destructive interference. Three primary ANC architectures have been developed: feedforward (FF [? ? ]), feedback (FB [? ? ]), and hybrid (H) systems. A typical FFANC configuration uses a reference microphone to capture the unwanted noise, a secondary source to emit antinoise, and an error microphone placed at the control point. This configuration allows broadband noise reduction, but only for components that are detectable by the reference microphone. In contrast, FBANC uses only an error microphone, limiting their effectiveness to narrowband or periodic noise signals that exhibit predictable characteristics. HANC systems combine the strengths of both FF and FB configurations to enhance noise reduction performance across a broader frequency range. A common limitation of these ANC systems is that they create a "zone of quiet" (ZoQ) centered around the error microphone, with noise attenuation constrained to that localized region. Moreover, the size of the ZoQ is frequency-dependent, meaning that certain frequency bands may remain unaffected by ANC [? ? ? ].
To address this spatial limitation, virtual sensing techniques have been proposed [? ? ? ? ? ? ? ? ? ? ? ? ? ]. These methods estimate the noise signal at a desired location without physically placing a microphone there. This can be achieved by estimating the signal at the desired location by using the signal at an error microphone location far from the desired location. Several methods have been proposed for virtual sensing, however, this paper focuses on the remote microphone-based virtual sensing (RMVS) that uses a compensation filter to model the relationship between the error microphone and the desired location.
Previous work has incorporated RMVS into HANC systems [? ], allowing effective noise reduction in environments with multiple sources, such as factories, without requiring microphones near human operators. In such scenarios, broadband mechanical noise is typically suppressed via FF control, while narrowband or tonal noise is managed through FB control. However,

conventional RMVS-based HANC systems apply a single correction filter associated with the signal from the reference microphone. In the presence of multiple noise sources, this approach becomes inadequate: a single filter cannot capture the distinct paths and contributions from each source. Furthermore, the error microphone receives a composite signal that blends all source components, preventing the system from isolating individual noise contributions--resulting in suboptimal control performance.
To overcome these limitations, this paper proposes a novel active noise control system for RMVS with two dedicated compensation filters per noise source. The proposed system can accurately estimate the desired signals at the desired location by creating compensation filters for each of multiple noise sources. Two compensation filters are created for each primary path from the noise source to the error microphone and the desired location in feed-forward and feedback ANC. By using an additional adaptive filter, the desired signals for each noise source are separated from the mixed signal from multiple noise sources acquired microphone. By applying the appropriate compensation filters to each separated component, the system reconstructs the target signal at the desired location with high accuracy.
The paper is organized as follows: Section 2 reviews the FFANC system using RMVS, followed by a description of the conventional HANC system using RMVS. Section 3 presents the proposed dual-compensation filter architecture. Section 4 provides a performance evaluation based on simulations using measured impulse responses. Section 5 concludes the paper and outlines directions for future work.
2. Conventional system
Virtual sensing aims to reduce unwanted noise at a specified target position without physically placing a microphone at that point. Existing techniques can be broadly categorized into two approaches: (i) array-based extrapolation methods [? ] that do not require pre-estimation, and (ii) methods that rely on pre-estimated transfer functions. While the former approach leverages dense microphone arrays to estimate spatial sound fields, it is often cost-prohibitive and impractical in real-world applications due to the large number of sensors required. Therefore, this study adopts the latter approach based on pre-estimated transfer functions.
Three microphones are mainly used for virtual sensing: (1) a reference microphone to acquire the primary noise signal, (2) a microphone placed at

the target location--referred to as the virtual microphone--used only during the tuning stage, and (3) an error microphone positioned at a distance from the desired (virtual microphone) location. The virtual sensing process consists of two stages: a tuning stage for estimating system characteristics and a control stage for real-time noise reduction. The purpose of RMVS is to estimate the signal at the virtual microphone position using the signal measured at the error microphone. During the tuning stage, a microphone is temporarily placed at the desired location of noise reduction, and a compensation filter is estimated to model the relationship between the virtual microphone and the error microphone. In the subsequent control stage, the compensation filter is applied to the error microphone signal to estimate the desired signal at the desired location, enabling noise reduction without the need for a physical microphone at that point.

3. Proposed system
To address the limitations described above, this paper proposes a HANC system based on RMVS that incorporates two dedicated compensation filters. The primary objective of the proposed system is to enable accurate estimation of the desired signal at the virtual microphone location, even in the presence of multiple noise sources. However, direct application of compensation filters to the signal observed at the error microphone is problematic, as this signal is a mixture of noise originating from both noise source 1 and

To overcome this challenge, the proposed system introduces an additional adaptive filter H(z) to decompose the mixed signal into its constituent components corresponding to each individual noise source. Once separated, the appropriate compensation filters are applied to each component, allowing precise reconstruction of the desired signal at the virtual microphone position.

4.3. Evaluation of the signal separation performance of the additional adaptive filter H(z)
Fig. 7 presents an evaluation of the proposed system in terms of the separation performance of the additional adaptive filter H(z). Under this ground truth scenario, Fig. 7(a) shows that the proposed system achieves approximately 30 dB of noise reduction. Moreover, Fig. 7(b) highlights that the green line outperforms the red line by approximately 5-10 dB in the frequency range around 500, 600, and 700 Hz--regions dominated by energy from noise source 2. The performance gap arises because, in the practical case (red line), the second term in Eq.

Recursive Least-Squares Algorithms for the
Identiﬁcation of Low-Rank Systems
Camelia Elisei-Iliescu, Constantin Paleologu, Member , IEEE, Jacob Benesty, Cristian Stanciu, Cristian Anghel,
and Silviu Ciochin ˘a, Senior Member , IEEE
Abstract—The recursive least-squares (RLS) adaptive ﬁlter is
an appealing choice in many system identiﬁcation problems.
The main reason behind its popularity is its fast convergence
rate. However, this algorithm is computationally very complex,
which may make it useless for the identiﬁcation of long length
impulse responses, like in echo cancellation. Computationally
efﬁcient versions of the RLS algorithm, like those based on
the dichotomous coordinate descent (DCD) iterations or QR
decomposition techniques, reduce the complexity, but still have
to face the challenges related to long length adaptive ﬁlters
(e.g., convergence/tracking capabilities). In this paper, we focus
on a different approach to improve the efﬁciency of the RLS
algorithm. The basic idea is to exploit the impulse response
decomposition based on the nearest Kronecker product and low-
rank approximation. In other words, a high-dimension system
identiﬁcation problem is reformulated in terms of low-dimension
problems, which are combined together . This approach was re-
cently addressed in terms of the Wiener ﬁlter, showing appealing
features for the identiﬁcation of low-rank systems, like real-world
echo paths. In this paper, besides the development of the RLS
algorithm based on this approach, we also propose a variable
regularized version of this algorithm (using the DCD method to
reduce the complexity), with improved robustness to double-talk.
Simulations are performed in the context of echo cancellation and
the results indicate the good performance of these algorithms.
Index Terms—Adaptive ﬁlter, echo cancellation, low-rank ap-
proximation, nearest Kronecker product, recursive least-squares
(RLS) algorithm, regularization, system identiﬁcation.
I. I NTRODUCTION
In many system identiﬁcation problems, the unknown sys-
tem can be modeled as a ﬁnite impulse response ﬁlter with
a large number of coefﬁcients, which raises additional chal-
lenges for the adaptive ﬁlter used for this purpose. In this con-
text, the echo cancellation application [1]– [3] represents one
of the most popular examples. In both network and acoustic
echo cancellation scenarios, the echo paths can have excessive
lengths in time (e.g., up to hundreds of milliseconds), which
further involves long length adaptive ﬁlters, with hundreds (or
even thousands) of coefﬁcients. Moreover, the echo paths may
rapidly change during the connection, which further implies
to model and track time-varying systems. All these aspects
inﬂuence the convergence rate and tracking of the adaptive
ﬁlter that models the echo path.

In terms of the convergence features, the recursive least-
squares (RLS) algorithm [4], [5] could represent an appealing
choice. However, its main limitation is related to the high
computational complexity, which becomes prohibitive in case
of long length adaptive ﬁlters. Several methods to reduce the
computational amount of the RLS algorithm can be found
in the literature, which lead to the so-called fast or efﬁcient
versions. Among them, the dichotomous coordinate descent
(DCD) algorithm [6]– [8] proposed by Zakharov and co-
authors represents one of the most interesting and useful
approaches. Due to its good performance and moderate com-
putational complexity, the resulting RLS-DCD algorithm was
successfully applied in the context of different applications,
e.g., [9]– [17], including echo cancellation. Another appealing
category of fast RLS algorithms is the family based on QR
decomposition (QRD) techniques. Here, we can mention the
QRD-based least-squares lattice (QRD-LSL) algorithm [4] and
different versions of it [18], which are numerically stable and
match the performance of the regular RLS algorithm.
Nevertheless, even if the computational complexity of the
fast RLS algorithms is moderate and could be suitable for
practical implementations, the problem of dealing with long
length adaptive ﬁlters is still a critical issue in system identi-
ﬁcation contexts like echo cancellation. In this framework, it
would be highly interesting and useful to take advantage of
the characteristics of the impulse response. The ﬁnal goal is
to transform a high-dimension system identiﬁcation problem
into “smaller” problems (i.e., shorter ﬁlters) that can be further
combined to match the original purpose. The expected gain
would be twofold, i.e., in terms of both performance and
complexity. It is well known that the echo paths are sparse in
nature, i.e., only a relatively small percentage of the impulse
response components have a signiﬁcant magnitude while the
rest are zero or small. This property inspired the development
of the so-called proportionate algorithms, e.g., see [19]– [25]
and the references therein. However, as will be shown in the
following, our approach is not only limited to the sparseness
characteristic of the impulse response, but it is more related to
an efﬁcient way to perform its decomposition. In other words,
the sparsity could help but it is not the only factor.
In our work, we propose to address the previous idea based
on a Kronecker product decomposition [26] together with
low-rank approximation. These techniques were previously
exploited in different frameworks. Most often, they are related
to the processing of bilinear/trilinear forms based on tensor
decomposition and modelling [27]– [46]. Consequently, they
led to appealing solutions within a wide range of important

applications, like big data, machine learning, and source

developed in this context, e.g., [33], [34], [43], [44], did not
involve low-rank approximations. Therefore, they face a major
limitation related to the particular (quasi-periodic) form of the
impulse response to be identiﬁed. While this form resembles
(to some extent) a channel with echoes, it is far from the very
different natures of realistic echo paths.
Recently, in [45], we proposed an iterative Wiener ﬁlter
based on an optimal (low-rank) approximation that involves
the Kronecker product decomposition. Basically, the linear
system identiﬁcation problem was addressed by exploiting
the impulse response decomposition using the nearest Kro-
necker product [26]. Then, we took advantage of the low-
rank approximation of this model, which ﬁts very well for
the identiﬁcation of low-rank systems. Thus, a problem of
high dimension was reformulated and low-dimension problems
were combined together. Due to this feature, the proposed
iterative Wiener ﬁlter is able to obtain accurate results using
less amount of data. The results presented in [45] indicate
that this solution is generally applicable for the identiﬁcation
of different impulse responses, like real-world network and
acoustic echo paths. In this context, we should mention that
such low-rank systems can be encountered in many other
frameworks, like digital TV transmission [47], satellite-linked
communications [48], radar systems and underwater com-
munications [17], [49], beamformers and microphone arrays
[46], [50] among others. However, due to its limitations (e.g.,
estimation of the statistics, matrix inversion, time-invariant
framework), the Wiener ﬁlter is not a practical solution in
real-time applications and time-variant environments, where
adaptive ﬁlters are the ideal choice.
In this paper, we make a ﬁrst step toward this direction
by proposing RLS-based algorithms using the decomposition
approach presented in [45]. At this point, the main goal is
not to derive a “fast” RLS-based algorithm, but to outline and
exploit the features of the proposed approach, i.e., impulse re-
sponse decomposition and low-rank approximation. Therefore,
in the current work, we mainly illustrate this idea following the
line of the conventional RLS algorithm. Nevertheless, in future
works, it would be highly interesting and useful to explore this
approach in the framework of the fast versions, like the RLS-
DCD and QRD-LSL algorithms. However, in this case, we
should take into account the particular structure of the input
data and the additional related challenges.
Following this introduction, the rest of the paper is or-
ganized as follows. Section II summarizes the background
from [45], which is required for the upcoming developments.
The RLS algorithm based on the nearest Kronecker product
decomposition is derived in Section III; some parts of this
section were introduced in [51]. In Section IV, a variable reg-
ularized version of this algorithm is presented, with improved
robustness to double-talk (which is a critical issue in echo
cancellation); this algorithm also involves the DCD iterations
to reduce the computational complexity related to the matrix
inversion operation. Simulation results are provided in Sec-
tion V, in the context of echo cancellation. Finally, Section VI
concludes this work and outlines several perspectives.

highly interesting to extend these ideas in case of the fast

IV. V ARIABLE REGULARIZED RLS-NKP A LGORITHM
A very important issue in echo cancellation is the double-
talk robustness provided by the adaptive algorithm [1], [2].
In a double-talk scenario, the talkers on both sides speak
simultaneously, so that the near-end speech acts like a large
level disturbance to the adaptive ﬁlter. For this purpose, the
RLS-based algorithms should use a value of the forgetting
factor very close to 1 [56]. On the other hand, a high value of
the forgetting factor signiﬁcantly affects the tracking capability
of the algorithm. However, the double-talk robustness of
the RLS algorithm can also be controlled in terms of the
regularization parameter [14], [57].
At ﬁrst glance, the regularization term is required in matrix
inversion when this matrix is ill-conditioned, especially in the
initialization stage of the algorithm. For example, as we can
notice in Table I, the RLS-NKP algorithm uses regularization
constants only in the initialization stage, but the inﬂuence
of these terms is negligible (for t large enough) due to the
forgetting factors. Nevertheless, the role of the regularization
parameters could be of great importance in practice, since
regularization is a must in all ill-posed problems, especially in
the presence of additive noise (or the near-end signal, in the
context of echo cancellation) [58]– [60].
In this section, we focus on the regularized RLS algo-
rithm [5] and develop a version based on the nearest Kronecker
product decomposition. Also, following the idea from [60],
a method to select the optimal regularization parameters is
presented, so that the algorithm could behave well in noisy
conditions and especially during double-talk. Moreover, using
a proper estimation of the SNR, the ﬁnal solution results in
the form of a variable regularized algorithm. Finally, in order
to reduce the computational amount required by the matrix
inversion operation, the DCD algorithm [6], [7] is involved.

physical property that underpins the low-rank approximation
(with P ≪ L2). However, even in this case, a reasonable
attenuation of the misalignment [e.g., around −20 dB in
Fig. 4(b)] can be obtained using a relatively small value of P
(as compared to L2). Overall, these preliminary results validate
the approximation behind (11) and justify the motivation that
led to the development of the proposed RLS-NKP algorithms.

VI. C ONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORKS
The system identiﬁcation problems become more chal-
lenging in case of long length impulse responses, like in
echo cancellation. In terms of the performance criteria, these
challenges mainly refer to the convergence rate, tracking, and
complexity. Even the fast (i.e., low-complexity) versions of
the RLS algorithm have to cope with the convergence/tracking
issues associated with long length adaptive ﬁlters.
In this paper, we have addressed these challenges from a dif-
ferent perspective, by exploiting an efﬁcient decomposition of
the impulse response based on the nearest Kronecker product
and the related low-rank approximation. The idea was recently
presented in [45], in terms of the Wiener ﬁlter. In order to
provide a more practical solution for low-rank system identi-
ﬁcation problems, we have proposed in this work several RLS-
based algorithms following this approach, namely RLS-NKP .
Besides the basic version of this algorithm, we have developed
a variable regularized RLS-NKP algorithm, which also uses
DCD iterations to avoid matrix inversion operations (namely
VR-RLS-NKP-DCD). As shown in simulations, the RLS-NKP
algorithm outperforms its regular RLS-based counterparts in
terms of tracking capabilities; this is due to the fact that the
RLS-NKP algorithm uses two shorter ﬁlter of length P L1 and
P L2 (with P < L 2), while the length of the regular RLS-based
ﬁlters is L = L1L2. Besides, for P ≪ L2 (which is usually the
case), the computational complexity of the RLS-NKP is lower
as compared to the regular RLS algorithm. In addition, the VR-
RLS-NKP-DCD provides improved robustness to double-talk,
due to the practical estimate of the SNR within the algorithm.
Moreover, its computational complexity is lower as compared
to the RLS-NKP algorithm and almost similar (for P ≪ L2)
to other fast versions, like the QRD-LSL algorithm.
Due to the lack of space, a detailed theoretical analysis (in
terms of convergence and stability) of the proposed algorithms
is beyond the scope of the paper and it will be explored
in future works. In this context, it would be interesting to
interpret the problem in terms of a combination of adaptive
ﬁlters. Thus, a further analysis could reveal important aspects
that should be taken into account when setting the parameters
of these two ﬁlters, in order to improve the overall behavior.
In perspective, it would be highly interesting to apply the
proposed approach in case of other versions of the RLS algo-
rithm (in this paper, only the classical form was considered).
For example, we can focus on the fast versions of the RLS
algorithm, e.g., following the lines of the RLS-DCD and QRD-
LSL algorithms. Nevertheless, in this framework, we should
take into account the speciﬁc challenges related to the particu-
lar structure of the input data. On the other hand, a fast RLS-
NKP algorithm would represent a very useful ﬁnding, since
it could inherit the performance features of the decomposition
approach, together with the low complexity speciﬁc to the
fast versions. Also, it would be useful to extend these ideas
for multichannel algorithms, targeting speciﬁc applications
like stereophonic acoustic echo cancellation. Besides, the
Kronecker product decomposition could be further exploited in
this context, generalizing the approach for higher dimensions
(i.e., more than two ﬁlters). Finally, even if the performances
of the proposed RLS-NKP algorithms were illustrated in the
context of echo cancellation, it would be interesting to evaluate
their behavior in the framework of different other applications
that require the identiﬁcation of low-rank systems.

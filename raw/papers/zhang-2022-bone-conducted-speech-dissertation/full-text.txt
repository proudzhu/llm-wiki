Abstract
In recent years, much attention has been paid to bone-conducted (BC) speech for its superior noise-robustness and other potential features, the use of BC speech is explored for speech processing. However, many properties of BC speech are still unclear in various regions in speech processing such as pitch extraction, speech synthesis, noise reduction, speech enhancement, and so on.
In pitch extraction, some published papers indicate that BC speech can be used for its noise-robustness, however, these researches are not enough to clarify the properties and performance of BC speech for pitch extraction. In BC speech synthesis, there are very few works of BC speech synthesis as far as we know up to now. In the BC speech enhancement, there is still not a satisfactory approach to improve the quality of BC speech. Overall, many potential properties applications of BC speech are still unclear. In this paper, we propose several approaches to explore and analyze the performance, properties, and application of BC speech in pitch extraction and BC speech synthesis.
Pitch (fundamental period) or fundamental frequency is one of the most important and needful parameters to represent a speech signal. The pitch extraction is conducted from a speech signal through an air-conducted (AC) microphone. AC speech is often used for pitch extraction in noisy environments, however, it is corrupted by noise. Therefore, it is difficult to extract the pitch information accurately from such noisy AC speech. We propose two noise-robust pitch extraction methods combining AC speech with BC speech in noise environments. For the first proposed method (WACF-CEP), the WACF method is implemented for the AC speech input, while the CEP method is done for the BC speech input, and then a multiplication of the processed AC speech and the processed BC speech is implemented in several types of noise environments. For the second proposed method (WACF-WACF), WACF is utilized to process the AC and BC speech signals, respectively, and then a multiplication of the processed AC speech and the processed BC speech is implemented in noisy environments. In both two proposed methods, multiplication from two functions and the use of BC speech accelerate the true peak enhancement, resulting in a decrease in pitch extraction errors. Experimental results show superior performances of both proposed methods relative to the state-of-the-art method in all the types of noise.
Speech synthesis is an important region in speech processing. Many researchers explore the AC speech synthesis from the BC speech for the BC speech enhance-
13

ment. However, there are very few works for BC speech synthesis. Experience indicates that BC speech is more suitable to be used in many regions for its clarified and potential properties than that of AC speech. For this reason, we propose a simple approach for BC speech synthesis from the corresponding AC speech in real-time. However, when we synthesize BC speech, we have no promising ways to do so. Although some speech synthesis techniques are well known, we cannot optimistically rely on them due to the lack of knowledge about the inherent properties of BC speech. Therefore, we investigate speech synthesis for BC speech. We propose a BC speech synthesis approach based on least squares method and conduct a pitch extraction experiment using the synthesized BC speech. From the identification results, a synthesis filter is designed in which AC speech is utilized as the input signal. BC speech is generated from the synthesis filter as the output signal. The synthesis method to be considered for BC speech is simple and straightforward and applicable to AC speech we usually use. Therefore, we can utilize the BC speech synthesis as a preprocessing part of AC speech processing, thereby an advanced implementation by BC speech could be exhibited. By experiments, it is shown that the BC speech synthesis method is satisfactory. Filter model, order selection, and stability in the methodology are discussed and noise-robustness gained by the BC speech synthesis is also investigated. A pitch extraction experiment is also implemented using the synthetic BC speech. The experimental results show that pitch extraction from the synthetic BC speech provides more accurate performance than that from the corresponding AC speech.

Introduction
1.1 Production of Speech
Speech is the most common means of human communication. In daily life, humans usually use speech for the transmission of messages from a speaker to a listener. The brain of speaker sends messages through the nerves and transmit them to the listener through speech waves, then listener accepts the speech waves trough auditory system and transmit them to the brain of listener. Speech is a sound wave of air, which is generated random air fluctuations from the lungs through the vocal cords and vocal tract. According to the vibration of vocal track or not, the speech is defined as the voiced or unvoiced speech. The main components of human speech production system are: the lungs, trachea, vocal cords, larynx, pharynx, vocal track, oral cavity and nasal cavity. The components can be described as follows, the lungs generate the air by using the muscle force, and then the air is streaming through the vocal cords of larynx, where it can be modulated and transferred to the vocal tract. Depending on the activities of vocal folds, the air produces a quasi-periodic wave. On the other hand, vocal tract can be modeled as an acoustic tube with variable resonator. For both voiced and unvoiced generation, the vocal tract behaves as a filter, which provides the pressure wave from the vocal cords and then amplifies to lips or the nostrils. Speech is simply as an acoustic wave that is radiated from the system, when air is expelled from the lungs and the resulting flow of air is perturbed by a constriction in the vocal tract [50].
The production of speech can also be viewed as a filtering operation. In this operation, an excitation source excites a vocal tract filter, this is known as sourcefilter model which is shown in Fig. 1.1. In Fig. 1.1 the excitation source is the

1.2 Speech Pyramid and Applications of Speech Processing
With the previous brief introduction to speech signals and human speech communication models, we can use a technology pyramid of digital speech processing (DSP) to introduce the details for speech processing. Fig. 1.2 shows that we term the "speech pyramid" which presents a layered view of the basic DSP concepts. The bottom (first layer) of the pyramid includes the basic science, technology, and theory of DSP, speech generation (acoustic), linguistics (the sound code of speech), and the perception of sounds, syllables, words, sentences, and ultimately meaning. These basic areas of knowledge form the basis of speech signal processing, which is performed to convert speech signals into a form that is more conducive to obtaining the information contained therein.
The second and third layers of the pyramid we called the concept layer. The second layer in the speech pyramid includes a set of basic representations of speech signals. These representations include temporal representations (including the speech waveform itself), spectral representations (Fourier magnitudes and phases)

The third layer in the pyramid is concerned with integrating the various speech representations into algorithms that estimate the basic properties of speech signals. The algorithms in this layer are used for extracting speech features through the speech representations which are described in the second layer, various speech algorithms (known collectively as pitch extraction methods) facilitate the determination of pitch period (or pitch frequency), and a different set of algorithms (known collectively as formant estimation methods) can be used to estimate the set of vocal track resonances or formants for the speech section of interest. Pitch is an important parameter and accurate pitch extraction is a research goal in speech processing. Many applications of speech processing are based on accurate pitch information. This will be mentioned in the fourth layer of the pyramid.
The fourth or top layer in the pyramid is the set of end-user applications of speech processing. The top layer represents the payoff for the technology and consists of a range of applications including speech coding, speech synthesis, speech recognition and understanding, speaker verification and recognition, language translation, speech enhancement systems, speech speed-up, and slow-down

In these areas, the centrality of the digital representation is obvious, since the goal is to compress the digital waveform representation of speech into a lower bit rate representation. It is common to refer to this activity as "speech coding" or "speech compression." In general, this compression is achieved by combining DSP techniques with fundamental knowledge of the speech production, perception processes of speech [51]. Another large class of speech processing applications is concerned with the automatic extraction of information from the speech signal. Most such systems involve some sort of pattern matching. Speech synthesis is widely used in speech processing. The implementation of such applications is based on the information of speech which is described in the third layer in the pyramid such as pitch extraction [1-22], voiced/unvoiced speech detection [36], and so on.
1.3 Different Types of Speech and Microphone
In a natural hearing, sound waves are carried through the air (air conduction). Air conduction relies on the outer ear, middle ear, and inner ear. Cartilage conduction is a pathway in which sound signals are transmitted to the inner ear. This pathway is distinct from conventional sound pathways such as air or bone. Therefore, cartilage conduction is referred to as the third auditory pathway. Bone conduction is the conduction of sound to the inner ear primarily through the bones of the skull, allowing the hearer to perceive audio content without blocking the ear canal. Bone conduction transmission occurs constantly as sound waves through bone, specifically through the skull. For the microphones, some new microphones, such as bone-conductive microphones, throat microphones, and non-audible mur-

1.4 Bone-Conducted Speech
Last decade, a lot of attention has been paid to bone-conducted (BC) speech, one of those picked up from the human body. The head or in-ear is often chosen for the location on the human body where a BC microphone is equipped, although BC speech is affected by the equipped locations. This is because the comparatively high quality of BC speech is obtained and it is easy to set up such types of BC microphones. Research about BC speech has a long history. In the 1950s, intensities of the voices to be picked up were exclusively discussed and some types of microphones were developed. After several decades, it was unveiled that BC speech is less sensitive to ambient noise than air-conducted (AC) speech measured by a standard microphone. This was timely matched to the requirement from the real world. Then, motivated by early works in the 2000s, where some techniques for quality improvement of BC speech were developed, BC speech has been often utilized for the purpose of speech enhancement in recent years. The intelligibility of BC speech is, in many cases, lower than that of AC speech, since the high-frequency components of BC speech are attenuated due to the transmission of voices on bones and skulls in the human body. However, state-of-the-art techniques would achieve quality improvement and noise reduction simultaneously for BC speech.
1.5 Motivations
Some researches indicate that BC speech has the potential to be inherently and effectively utilized in speech processing. However, the properties of BC speech are still unclear in some regions such as pitch extraction, speech synthesis, speech enhancement, and so on. In order to clarify the properties of BC speech, researchers are exploring how BC speech can be applied in speech processing systems like speech enhancement [29][30], pitch extraction [31][32], and so on. Pitch (fundamental period) or fundamental frequency is one of the most important parameters in speech processing. Pitch information is effectively embedded and utilized in speech-related processing systems. Therefore, the extraction of pitch informa-

tion from the speech signal is important for speech-related processing systems and applications, and numerous methods have been addressed [1]-[32]. In a noiseless environment, pitch extraction from BC speech leads to better performance as compared to the corresponding AC speech case [31]. However, the pitch extraction performance from BC speech in noisy environments is not known well except for a preliminary experimental result in [32]. For this reason, two pitch extraction approaches WACF-CEP and WACF-WACF are proposed and conducted in different types of noise environments. Proposed methods WACF-CEP and WACF-WACF utilize both AC and BC speeches as the input signals. However, when we synthesize such BC speech, we have no promising ways to do so. There are very few works of BC speech synthesis. Designing a BC synthesis filter for synthesizing the BC speech from AC speech in real-time should be needed in speech processing. For these reasons, we investigate speech synthesis for BC speech and propose a BC speech synthesis approach based on least squares method, and then conduct a pitch extraction experiment using the synthesized BC speech. From these researches, we clarify that BC speech can be utilized in pitch extraction and the synthesized speech can be utilized as a processing in speech processing such as noise reduction and pitch extraction.

In Chapter 3, a BC speech synthesis approach based on least squares method is proposed. Evaluation and pitch extraction experiments for the synthesized BC speech are implemented there. Pitch Extraction Approaches for BC Speech
2.1 Background of Pitch Extraction
Pitch (fundamental period) or fundamental frequency is one of the most important and needful parameters to represent a speech signal. Pitch information is effectively embedded and utilized in speech-related processing systems such as speech enhancement, speech coding, speech analysis, speaker identification, and so on. Therefore, the extraction of pitch information from the speech signal is crucial, and numerous methods have been addressed [1]-[32]. The extraction is conducted from a speech signal through an air-conducted (AC) microphone, which is referred to as the AC speech signal in this paper. However, AC speech is often used in noisy environments. In this case, AC speech is corrupted by noise. It is very difficult to extract the pitch information accurately from such noisy AC speech. One of the most important features of BC speech is its excellent noise-robustness. In this section, we analyze and explore the performance of BC speech in pitch extraction.
2.2 Conventional Pitch Extraction Methods
The auto-correlation function (ACF) method [1] is known to provide noise robustness in a wide band noise. For this reason, there exist several modified versions of the ACF method [2]-[9]. However, the ACF based methods are often affected by the formant characteristics in the AC speech signal, resulting in performance degradation.
21

Figure 2.1: Framework of pitch extraction
While the ACF-based methods are calculated in the time domain, the cepstrum (CEP) method [10] is done in the frequency domain. The CEP method is less affected by the formant characteristics. In a noiseless environment, the CEP method becomes an accurate pitch extractor. However, the CEP method is severely affected by a wide band noise, while it behaves as a good pitch extractor in a periodical noise such as car noise. Some variations of the CEP method also exist [11][12]. Extending the frequency domain methods, two state-of-the-art methods, PEFAC [13] and BaNa [14], have been derived recently. PEFAC estimates pitch by convolving its power spectral density in the log-frequency domain with a filter that sums the energy of the pitch harmonics while rejecting additive noise that has a smoothly varying power spectrum. Also, amplitude compression is applied before filtering to attenuate narrow-band noise components. BaNa is a noise resilient pitch detection method that has good performance in noisy environments. Speech harmonics are regularly spaced at integer multiples of the fundamental frequency. BaNa uses the Viterbi algorithm to search for harmonic peaks and determines the pitch from the candidates by calculating the ratios of harmonics. In [14][15], it is reported that BaNa provides the best performance in competitive methods that include PEFAC. However, the performance degrades in highly noisy environments even for BaNa. Current research work includes a deep learning approach for pitch extraction [16]-[18]. However, an extremely long length of speech data is required to learn the corresponding network architecture in implementing such approaches in practice. Although particle filters [19] are applicable for pitch extraction, they are utilized only in white Gaussian noise case. The instantaneous fundamental frequency estimation approach [20] and its extended version [21] are also applicable to white Gaussian noise case. Although the signal frequency filtering approach [22] is applicable to a variety of noise characteristics, it may not be tolerant of situations at a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) less than 0 dB.

BC Speech Synthesis Based on Least Squares Method
3.1 Background of BC Speech Synthesis
Speech synthesis is an important research task in speech processing. Most researchers explore the AC speech synthesis from the BC speech for the BC speech enhancement. However, there are very few works for BC speech synthesis. Experience indicates that BC speech is more suitable to be used in many regions for its clarified and potential properties than that of AC speech. Some papers report that the use of BC speech leads to more accurate rates of speaker verification in noiseless environments, where AC speech is utilized together [52][53]. BC speech behaves so as to compensate for that of AC speech, leading to balanced equal error rates. For this reason, we propose a simple approach for BC speech synthesis from the corresponding AC speech in real-time. However, when we synthesize BC speech, we have no promising ways to do so. Although some speech synthesis techniques are well known, we cannot optimistically rely on them due to the lack of knowledge about the inherent properties of BC speech. Therefore, we investigate speech synthesis for BC speech.

3.9 Noise-Robustness Investigation
BC speech has much better noise-robustness than that of AC speech (giving about 10dB SNR gain) [46] 1 . BC speech is generated from the vibration of glottis through the bone and some muscle tissue and picked up by a BC microphone. The BC speech generation is basically assumed to be conducted inside the human body. The IIR filter designed generates noiseless BC speech from noiseless AC speech. However, when the environmental noise (AC noise) is picked up by a BC microphone together, the noise-induced in the BC speech (BC noise) should be discussed. The process of BC noise generation is outside the human body, which may be different from that of BC speech generation. However, we investigated the designed IIR filter for BC speech generation from the aspect of noise-robustness.

Conclusion and Future Work
In this paper, we propose several approaches to explore and analyze the performance and properties of BC speech in pitch extraction and BC speech synthesis, respectively.
In the pitch extraction part, we have presented two frameworks for noise-robust pitch extraction in which AC and BC speeches are utilized simultaneously. Two combination example is that the WACF is calculated for the AC speech signal, while the CEP is done for the BC speech signal and another one is WACF is calculated for the AC and BC speeches, respectively. The two functions are multiplied to create a peak characteristic for pitch extraction. From some analytical discussions and noise suppression effect in BC speech, it has been shown that the proposed method leads to accurate pitch extraction even in highly noisy environments without increasing computational complexity. Experiments have supported to validate a superior performance of two proposed methods relative to the state-of-the-art method.
In the BC speech synthesis part, we have set out to generate BC speech from AC speech through a low-order IIR filter model. This is recognized as a synthesis method for BC speech from AC speech. This idea was motivated by the importance of BC speech in the field of speech processing where the use of BC speech is more beneficial and effective than that of AC speech. We considered designing the synthesis filter through the least squares method in the time domain, and successfully obtained it from the recorded AC and BC speeches. Experimental results showed that the BC speech synthesized by the resulting filter is close to the original BC speech from both points of view in the time and frequency domains. LAR distance evaluation and listening test validated that the synthesized BC speech is similar
86

87
to the original BC speech and that the synthesis methodology is satisfactory. In addition, noise-robustness gained by the BC speech synthesis was investigated. A pitch extraction experiment is conducted for investigating the application of the synthetic BC speech. Experimental results show that pitch extraction from the synthetic BC speech provides more accurate performance than that from the corresponding AC speech. Overall, the results indicate that BC speech and synthetic BC speech which have similar features with real BC speech can be utilized in speech processing.
In this paper, we proposed two methods to improve pitch extraction accuracy. We hope that the proposed methods can be utilized widely in the application based on accurate pitch information. In the other region for speech analysis, we consider exploring applications that used BC speech such as voiced and unvoiced part detection. In the speech synthesis, the designed filter synthesizes the BC speech from AC speech, successfully. We consider that this synthesis filter can be widely used to synthesize BC speech simply through a smartphone or computer and the synthesized BC speech can be useful for pitch extraction, speaker verification, and so on.

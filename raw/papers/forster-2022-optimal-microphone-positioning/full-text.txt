Introduction
As more and more people live in urban areas, constant ex-
posure to noise becomes an ever-growing problem. Typ-
ical scenarios where people face noise pollution are, e.g.,
means of transport, construction or industrial noise [1].
In recent years, active noise cancellation (ANC) head-
phones have become increasingly popular since they can
eﬀectively attenuate unwanted noise over a broad fre-
quency range. They typically comprise an internal er-
ror microphone and an external reference microphone.
The use of multiple external reference microphones in
the feedforward ﬁlter structure promises an increase of
active attenuation by exploiting the spatial properties of
the incoming noise.
However, when equipping a headphone with multiple ref-
erence microphones, their positioning plays a crucial role.
The positioning of the internal error microphone is well
studied [2], but literature on reference microphone posi-
tioning is sparse. Meng et al.[3] have analyzed three po-
sitions of reference microphones in a single-channel feed-
forward ANC system and related the results to particle-
velocity measurements on the shell of an ANC head-
phone. Alternative algorithms for microphone position
selection are based on the multiple coherence function
(MCF), which deﬁnes the theoretical upper bound of
achievable performance [4], and have been proposed in
patents [5, 6]. The MCF is derived in frequency domain
and does therefore not account for system causality, an
essential requirement for practical use.
We propose the use of a multi-channel mean square error-
based time-domain cost function (CF) (MSE-TD CF) for
an approximated diﬀuse sound ﬁeld resulting in a multi-
channel Wiener ﬁlter. In contrast to the MCF, the re-
sulting FIR ﬁlter coeﬃcients are causal. In order to an-
alyze a large number of possible microphone positions,
we equip a commercially available ANC headphone with
10 additional micro-electro-mechanical system (MEMS)
microphones. Based on that, we compare the increase
in performance due to these microphones in a simulated,
approximated diﬀuse sound ﬁeld. As evaluation criteria
(ECs) we use our proposed CF and the MCF based on
path measurements in our audio lab.

Conclusion
In this work, we analyzed the optimal microphone po-
sitions of a MISO ANC headphone prototype. We pre-
sented a directional multi-channel ﬁlter design for feed-
forward ANC and adapted this design to deduce a simu-
lated, approximated diﬀuse sound ﬁeld consisting of mul-
tiple mutually uncorrelated noise sources in a horizontal
ring conﬁguration. In order to evaluate the potential
increase of the ANC performance due to additional mi-
crophones we equipped a commercially available head-
phone with 10 additional MEMS microphones and com-
pared the performance with the estimate by the MCF.
The evaluation indicates that for the simulated scenario
the active gain can be decreased by up to 9 dB when us-
ing all ipsilateral microphones compared to using only
one microphone. Contralateral microphones don’t sig-
niﬁcantly contribute to the performance as they would
require acausal ﬁlters to compensate for the large group
delay.

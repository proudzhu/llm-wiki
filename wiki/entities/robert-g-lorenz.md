# Robert G. Lorenz

**Affiliation**: Beceem Communications, Inc., Santa Clara, CA (at time of 2005 publication); previously SiRF Technology and Ashtech Inc.
**Role**: Engineer / Vice President of Engineering
**Research Focus**: Space-time processing for wireless communications, numerical optimization, array signal processing, GPS receiver design.

## Background

Robert G. Lorenz (M'03) received the B.S. degree in electrical engineering and computer science from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1987 and the Ph.D. degree in electrical engineering from Stanford University, Stanford, CA, in 2003.

In 1987, he co-founded **Ashtech Inc.** (Sunnyvale, CA), a leading provider of high-accuracy Global Positioning System (GPS) surveying systems. He later served as Director of Engineering at **SiRF Technology** (San Jose, CA), where he led the development of high-sensitivity GPS receivers. At the time of the 2005 IEEE TSP paper, he was Vice President of Engineering at **Beceem Communications, Inc.** (Santa Clara, CA), leading the physical layer development of broadband wireless systems.

## Key Contributions

- **Robust Minimum Variance Beamforming** (IEEE TSP 2005, with [[entities/stephen-boyd|Stephen P. Boyd]]) — introduced the RMVB, casting robust MVDR beamforming with anisotropic ellipsoidal array-manifold uncertainty as a second-order cone program solvable by Lagrange multiplier methods; also developed novel Hadamard-product-of-ellipsoids calculus for multiplicative uncertainty propagation. See [[sources/lorenz-2005-robust-minimum-variance-beamforming|Lorenz & Boyd 2005]].
- Earlier related conference work: "An ellipsoidal approximation to the Hadamard product of ellipsoids" (ICASSP 2002) and "Robust beamforming in GPS arrays" (ION NTM 2002), both with Boyd.

# Stephen P. Boyd

**Affiliation**: Stanford University — Samsung Professor of Engineering, Professor of Electrical Engineering, Director of the Information Systems Laboratory
**Role**: Professor / Researcher
**Research Focus**: Convex optimization and its applications in control, signal processing, circuit design, machine learning, and finance.

## Background

Stephen P. Boyd (SM'97–F'99) received the AB degree in mathematics, *summa cum laude*, from Harvard University in 1980 and the Ph.D. degree in electrical engineering and computer science from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1985.

He is the **Samsung Professor of Engineering**, Professor of Electrical Engineering, and Director of the **Information Systems Laboratory** at Stanford University. His group developed the widely used convex optimization modeling frameworks **CVX** (MATLAB), **CVXPY** (Python), and **Convex.jl** (Julia), and the accompanying solvers. He is a Distinguished Lecturer of the IEEE Control Systems Society.

**Awards**: ONR Young Investigator Award, Presidential Young Investigator Award, 1992 AACC Donald P. Eckman Award, Perrin Award for Outstanding Undergraduate Teaching (Stanford Engineering), ASSU Graduate Teaching Award, and the 2003 AACC Ragazzini Education Award.

## Selected Publications

- *Convex Optimization* (with L. Vandenberghe, Cambridge University Press, 2004) — the standard graduate textbook on convex optimization.
- *Linear Matrix Inequalities in System and Control Theory* (with L. El Ghaoui, E. Feron, V. Balakrishnan, SIAM, 1994).
- *Linear Controller Design: Limits of Performance* (with C. Barratt, 1991).

## Key Contributions (relevant to this wiki)

- **Robust Minimum Variance Beamforming** (IEEE TSP 2005, with [[entities/robert-g-lorenz|Robert G. Lorenz]]) — applied robust convex optimization to array beamforming, formulating the RMVB as a second-order cone program and deriving an efficient Lagrange-multiplier solver. See [[sources/lorenz-2005-robust-minimum-variance-beamforming|Lorenz & Boyd 2005]].
- Foundational work on **semidefinite programming** (with Vandenberghe, 1996 SIAM Review) and **second-order cone programming** applications (with Lobo, Vandenberghe, Lebret, 1998), which underpin the [[concepts/socp-optimization|SOCP]] framework used in robust beamforming and robust ANC/PSZ design.

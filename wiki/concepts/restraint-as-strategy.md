# Restraint as Strategy

A distinctive commercial-strategy argument articulated by [[entities/wenfeng-liang|Liang Wenfeng]] (founder of [[entities/deepseek|DeepSeek]]) in his 2026 investor exchange meeting. The core claim: in a market as large as AI (potentially ~10% of global GDP), the player willing to take less profit wins. Restraint is not a sacrifice but a strategic advantage.

## The Argument

Liang's reasoning has four steps:

1. **AI is too large to monopolize** — "AI may eventually occupy 10% of human GDP. You cannot monopolize this; you must share, otherwise you will certainly not survive."
2. **Profit-taking is asymmetric in equilibrium** — if your vision is to take 5% of AI's GDP, you will be defeated by someone whose vision is to take only 1%. They will be defeated in turn by someone willing to take 0.1%. "Those who want more are defeated by those who want less."
3. **The floor is viability, the ceiling is competition** — taking too little means the company dies; taking too much means being defeated. The equilibrium is a "reasonable profit."
4. **Restraint increases the probability of reaching AGI** — by not maximizing short-term profit, the company can focus on the long-term research objective; by open-sourcing, it builds ecosystem and goodwill that compound over time.

## Concrete Expressions

At DeepSeek, restraint is expressed concretely in:

- **Open-source of frontier models** — including the strongest models; "I don't see what inevitable benefit closed-source brings"
- **Ten-month payback API pricing** — the price at which hardware cost is recovered in 10 months; roughly a 6× profit margin over hardware cost. "If we wanted 100× profit, open-source would hurt us. At 6×, it doesn't."
- **No C-end monetization push** — despite the Spring Festival 2025 viral moment, DeepSeek did not pivot to become "the next ByteDance"; C-end traffic is "sesame" compared to the AGI "watermelon"
- **No KPI / no考核 (appraisal) / minimal overtime** — restraint in organizational ambition; "research needs a relaxed environment"
- **Half formal work, half self-directed research time** — restraint in scheduling; employees choose half their work

## Distinction from Sacrifice

Liang explicitly distinguishes restraint from sacrifice:

> "对我来讲，克制是⼀种战略。就在于有时候你可以舍弃⼀些，来换更多其他的东⻄。"

Restraint is "a strategy" — by giving up some things, you trade for more. It is not asceticism; it is a calculated trade. Internal reaction to the API price cut from a higher level to 1/4 was "people in the company group chat were cheering" — restraint produces morale, not deprivation.

## Boundary Conditions

- The argument is specific to **AI as a market large enough that any single player cannot monopolize it**. It does not generalize to small markets.
- The argument assumes the company can survive on a "reasonable profit" — Liang notes that "if we just sell API, even with no further technical progress, that can support a listed company."
- The argument does not preclude commercialization — DeepSeek does have a B-end API business and C-end users; it just doesn't make them the primary objective.

# BCS-Guided Speech Enhancement

**BCS-Guided Speech Enhancement** is a multi-modal approach that fuses [[concepts/bone-conduction|bone conducted signals]] (BCS) from accelerometers with air-conducted microphone signals to improve speech enhancement performance, particularly for earbud-based voice assistants in noisy environments.

## Overview

Air-conducted microphones capture both target speech and ambient noise, while BCS accelerometers capture primarily the user's own voice but are band-limited (typically < 1–2 kHz). BCS-guided speech enhancement exploits the complementary strengths of both modalities: the noise immunity of BCS and the broadband spectral information of air-conducted signals.

## Architecture Pattern

The general pipeline for BCS-guided speech enhancement (Heitkaemper et al. 2025; patent 2026):

1. **BCS preprocessing**: Down-sample band-limited BCS STFT, then upscale via learned projection to match air-conducted signal dimensionality
2. **Feature fusion**: Concatenate upscaled BCS STFT coefficients with air-conducted STFT coefficients
3. **Mask estimation**: Process fused features through a stack of self-attention (Conformer) blocks to estimate an ideal ratio mask
4. **Signal reconstruction**: Apply mask to original STFT and reconstruct via inverse STFT

```
BCS → STFT → Down-sample → FF Upscale ──┐
                                          ├→ Conformer Stack → Mask → iSTFT → Enhanced Speech
Air mic → STFT ──────────────────────────┘
```

## Key Design Considerations

| Aspect | Design Choice | Rationale |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| BCS bandwidth | Down-sample then upscale | BCS is band-limited; upscaling via learned projection avoids zero-padding artifacts; cuts earbud-to-device transmission bandwidth to 6.25% at $f^{BCS}_{\max}=500$ Hz |
| Fusion strategy | Concatenation in STFT domain | Preserves full spectral information from both modalities; supports streaming |
| Mask type | Ideal ratio mask | Preserves original phase; avoids phase estimation errors |
| Architecture | Conformer (self-attention) | Captures long-range temporal dependencies in speech |
| Mic count | Single-channel air input | Model is agnostic to earbud microphone array size |

## Training Strategy

Two-stage training stabilizes learning:

1. **Pre-training**: Train Conformer stack + masking layer on spectral loss (L1 + L2 on ratio mask) + ASR loss, without BCS upscaling pathway
2. **Fine-tuning**: Add BCS upscaling projection layer and train end-to-end with same losses

The ASR loss compares encoder outputs for enhanced vs. target speech, directly optimizing for recognition accuracy rather than just signal quality.

The ICASSP 2025 paper specifies the joint objective $\lambda_{\text{ASR}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ASR}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{SI-SNR}}$ with $\lambda_{\text{ASR}} = 10^{-4}$ (ASR-loss à la Howard et al. 2021 + scale-invariant SNR loss), used during both pretraining (500k steps on ~50k h of simulated data) and finetuning (50k steps on ~6 h / 45 speakers of real earbud recordings). The simulated BCS is generated by passing reverberant speech (without added noise) through a 47-coefficient FIR low-pass filter with 500 Hz corner frequency, attenuated by 7–13 dB. Notably, no noise is added to the BCS during finetuning, since the deployed system only applies the enhancer for clean BCS (see VAD gating below).

## VAD Gating

BCS-based [[concepts/voice-activity-detection|VAD]] provides noise-robust speech detection. When VAD detects no speech, the ASR system bypasses enhancement and processes the raw noisy signal directly, avoiding enhancement artifacts during silence.

## Empirical Validation (Heitkaemper et al. 2025)

The ICASSP 2025 paper validates the architecture on >5 h of real earbud recordings across four conditions (Easy / Moderate / Hard / Dynamic; SPL 30–80 dBA, wind 0–6 m/s, Dynamic adds a walking interfering speaker):

- **BCS bandwidth reduction**: limiting the BCS to $f^{BCS}_{\max} = 500$ Hz (with the FF upscaling module) costs <1.5% absolute WER while cutting the earbud-to-device transmission bandwidth to 6.25% of the original — enabling the off-device (phone-side) enhancement use-case. The upscaling module improves WER across the 250 Hz–8 kHz range (except at 1 kHz), showing the model still exploits higher-frequency BCS information despite the low-pass characteristic of bone conduction.
- **VAD gating**: ~30% of realistic earbud recordings have uninformative BCS (no discernible speech patterns, mostly wind); enhancement actively *degrades* the ACS for examples with <60% detected keyword-BCS activity (~40% of the Hard set). Bypassing the enhancer when a pretrained ACS-style VAD detects no keyword-BCS activity improves Hard-set WER from 16.8 to 15.8 (500 Hz model).
- **vs. multi-channel enhancement**: at RTF 0.01 the BCS-guided single-channel model beats a state-of-the-art multi-channel enhancer (TfCleanformer "McEnhancer S", finetuned for earbuds) on 3 of 4 sets and dramatically outperforms the 39× costlier large variant on the Dynamic set (WER 10.9 vs 16.4); in the hardest conditions (interfering noise or wind ≥ 6 m/s) relative WER reduction exceeds 15%.

## Comparison with Related Approaches

| Approach | BCS Usage | Architecture | Output |
|----------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **SEANet** (Tagliasacchi 2020) | Raw waveform concatenation with audio | Wave-to-wave UNet (1D conv) | Waveform (via GAN) |
| **DC-CRN + AFF** (Wang 2022) | Attention mask soft-selects AC/BC, concatenated with originals | Densely-connected CRN + BLSTM | Complex spectral mapping |
| **BCS-guided SE** (Heitkaemper 2025) | Band-limited to 500 Hz, upscaled, concatenated with air STFT | Conformer | Ratio mask → iSTFT |
| **DenGCAN** (Kuang 2024) | iAFF coarse-then-refined fusion of STFTs | Densely gated conv + sConformer | Complex ratio mask → iSTFT |
| **Sub-band + full-band** (Kuang 2025) | Early iAFF (ERB energies + full-band spectra) + intermediate iAFF (embeddings) | Small ERB enc-dec + large spectral enc-dec, grouped sConformer bottleneck | 32 ERB gains → deep filtering (two-step) |
| **ATFA Dual-Mask** (Liu 2025) | Shared-conv pre-fusion + concat | Dilated DenseNet + ATFA + AHA | Dual real masks (AC + BC) summed |
| **MBENet** (Zhang 2025) | Bandwidth-extended BC (CRN module, multi-task) | Shared-weight band-split (31 bands) + dual-axis LSTM | Complex mask on AC (TSE) |
| **VibOmni** (He 2025) | IMU vibration upscaled (BCF aug.) | Dual-encoder DPRNN | Spectrogram |
| **Whisphone** (Fukumoto 2025) | In-ear MEMS captures occlusion BC | Separate channel | Direct voice input |
| **MDFNet** (Wang 2025) | Multi-modal deep filtering: separate coefficient sets $\mathbf{c}^a$, $\mathbf{c}^b$ for AC and BC spectra (low band); ERB gains on AC only (high band) | UNet-like encoder + grouped GRU, two offline decoders | Concatenated low-band filtered + high-band gained spectrum; PESQ 3.21, CER 24.8% |
| **OVAD** (Masilamani 2024) | Accelerometer for speech detection | VAD only | Binary speech flag |

Wang 2022's attention-based fusion (AFF) — an attention score soft-selecting between AC and BC features before concatenation — is an early precursor to the learned-fusion strategies in later work: Kuang 2024's [[concepts/iterative-attentional-feature-fusion|iAFF]] and Liu 2025's [[concepts/adaptive-time-frequency-attention|ATFA]]. Its ablation showed that fusion by simple addition loses single-modal information (−5.3% STOI), motivating the preserve-both-concatenate design that later architectures retain.

## Fusion Inside the Filter (Wang et al. 2025)

Every system in the table above fuses modalities in a *feature space* and then predicts a mask or filter. [[sources/wang-2025-multi-modal-deep-filtering-bc|Wang, Kuang, Li, Yang, Bai & Liu 2025]] break that pattern: their MDFNet adopts the [[concepts/multi-modal-deep-filtering|multi-modal deep filtering]] formulation $\hat{\mathbf{S}} = \sum_j \mathbf{c}^a(j)\mathbf{X}^a_a(k-j) + \sum_j \mathbf{c}^b(j)\mathbf{X}^b(k-j)$, so the AC and BC weighting is expressed *by* the complex filter coefficients rather than by a fusion module. The same parameter set degenerates to a single-modal system when $\mathbf{c}^b = 0$, which the authors exploit as their ablation: AC-DFNet scores PESQ 2.37 / STOI 0.69 / CER 57.0% versus 3.21 / 0.79 / 24.8% for MDFNet. Unlike DenGCAN or the Kuang 2025 sub-band/full-band model, there is no attention or gating block anywhere in the fusion path.

A second structural departure is the **frequency-asymmetric fusion**: the high band uses ERB gains applied only to AC (BC has nothing there to fuse), while the low band filters both modalities at full resolution. The models above instead fuse across the whole band, even where BC contributes little.

The trade-off shows in the results: on the same ABCS corpus, MDFNet beats the older time-domain MFCN decisively but trails the 3.79M-parameter MTINet on PESQ/STOI/ESTOI (3.21/0.79/0.69 vs. 3.29/0.91/0.84), while *winning* on ASR (24.8% vs. 27.3% CER). The paper attributes the ASR win to operating in the complex domain without convolutional feature learning or reconstruction, which preserves spectral detail. No parameter count, MACs, or RTF is reported, so the "lightweight" claim cannot be checked against the rest of this table.

## Robustness to Sensor Failure

Practical wearables suffer intermittent BC sensor invalidity (loose contact, jaw motion). Most fusion models trained only on valid-channel data degrade *worse than the surviving channel alone* when one sensor fails — they amplify the dead channel's noise into the output. The [[concepts/sensor-failure-robust-fusion|sensor-failure robust fusion]] discipline addresses this through:

- **Random modality dropout during training** (Liu 2025 "Special Training" — p=0.2 per channel)
- **Per-modality output heads** (dual-mask) that allow the network to suppress a dead channel
- **Multi-axis attention** ([[concepts/adaptive-time-frequency-attention|ATFA]]) providing architectural robustness even without dropout training

For the *continuous* distortion case (wind-corrupted but present BCS), Heitkaemper et al. 2025 take an inference-time route: VAD-based detection of uninformative BCS with hard bypass of the enhancer, rather than training-time robustness.

### Modality dominance as a fusion-level failure

A third robustness dimension appears in Wu et al. 2026: even when both sensors are healthy, fusion can collapse onto a single modality because at low SNR the BC branch is the *easier* optimisation target and its gradients dominate the shared stream. Their DBMIF framework counters this with three stages of [[concepts/modality-dominance|modality balancing]] — iterative channel-attention fusion ([[concepts/deep-iterative-attention-fusion|DIAF]]), bidirectional cross-branch gating ([[concepts/cross-branch-gated-interaction|CBGI]]), and a weight-tied equilibrium bottleneck ([[concepts/deep-balanced-interaction|DBI]]) — reaching 12.42 dB SI-SDR / 2.70 PESQ on the 100-speaker ABCS corpus against DenGCAN (10.75 / 1.98) and DC-CRN (10.18 / 2.62), with downstream CER of 15.84% (Whisper) and 12.07% (WeNet). Unlike the video-domain BCS-guided systems above, DBMIF is time-domain, GAN-trained, and operates on 4 [[concepts/pseudo-qmf-bank|PQMF]] subbands, so its fusion weights can be frequency-selective (BC for the lowest band, AC for the upper bands).
